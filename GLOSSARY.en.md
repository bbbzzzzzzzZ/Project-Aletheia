# Project Aletheia English Glossary

> This is a selective English glossary for public-facing materials. The Chinese [GLOSSARY.md](GLOSSARY.md) remains the primary working glossary.

## Core terms

| Term | Working meaning | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Person | A subject of experience, need, action, relation, and self-direction. | Not reducible to occupation, role, ability, contribution, productivity, diagnosis, or status. |
| Need | A subject’s felt lack, difficulty, desire, direction, or claim as it moves through interpretation and possible coordination. | Not identical to market demand, preference, entitlement, or command. |
| Need attribution | The judgment of whom a specific need primarily serves or belongs to: a person, organization, role-system, public, or mixed layer. | Not decided merely by who suffers, speaks, or requests first. |
| Private need | A need primarily serving a person’s own life purpose or preference. | Others may voluntarily respond, but the need does not automatically become public responsibility. |
| Public need claim | A claim that a minimum guarantee, response capacity, or shared condition should be maintained by the public. | A claim is not yet a confirmed public need and does not itself create budget, agency, task, or authority. |
| Public need | A need recognized through a contestable and revisable public justification process as belonging to the public as a condition of shared life. | Does not mean every member has the same duty or that the current sufferer is responsible for the whole system. |
| Public responsibility | The field of judgments concerning public necessity, public need attribution, institutional organization and maintenance, limited mutual public responsibility, and specific public task obligations. | Must specify which layer is meant; it is not a single automatic duty. |
| First responsibility / need-attribution responsibility | The responsibility of the subject to whom the need belongs not to externalize the need’s existence, boundary, reasonable cost, and continued bearing as a whole. | Not personal blame, direct execution, total responsibility, resource authority, or task obligation. |
| Basic guarantee | Minimum conditions for life, safety, dignity, and basic social participation, such as food, shelter, basic medical care, education, personal safety, and necessary services. | Not conditional on current labor, contribution, obedience, or public task performance. |
| Basic right | A normative standing a person has without needing task authorization, including expression, dissent, remedy, basic guarantees, and procedural access. | Not created by institutions as a reward; procedures may coordinate exercise but cannot become the source of the right. |
| Factual power | Significant or sustained asymmetric control over options, rules, resources, data, identity gates, evaluation, agenda, or remedies, whether or not formally authorized. | Existence does not prove legitimacy; ordinary persuasion or equal negotiation is not automatically factual power. |
| Authorized authority | Limited authority granted through traceable procedure to affect others, public rules, data, or resources within a specific task or public function. | Must define object, scope, action, resource, duration, oversight, review, and exit; not equivalent to ability. |
| Responsibility | Traceable attribution for requests, commitments, roles, judgments, decisions, authorizations, actions, and effects. | Not personal-status punishment, obedience, or authority by itself. |
| Limited mutual public responsibility | The principle that members with relevant real capacity may have bounded, fair, reviewable responsibilities to help maintain public necessary conditions. | Does not automatically generate a task, uniform hours, permanent standby, or loss of basic guarantees. |
| Specific public task obligation | A concrete task obligation formed under public responsibility only after public need, institutional prior responsibility, fair distribution, capacity fit, reasonable task conditions, alternatives, exemptions, appeal, and review are satisfied. | Not generated by membership, benefit, ability registration, or institutional convenience alone. |
| Task envelope | A complete boundary around a task: need source, affected persons, goal, non-goals, criteria, roles, capacity, place, time, compensation, resources, data, command relation, risk, third-party effects, authority, prohibitions, oversight, appeal, pause, change, handover, and exit. | A task envelope does not mean anyone has already committed or been authorized. |
| Task commitment | A person’s informed acceptance of a limited role after understanding the concrete task, conditions, risks, and exit rules. | Matching, contact, or ability is not commitment. |
| Execution authority / task authority | Limited authority to act on actions, resources, data, or persons within a specific task. | Cannot be inferred directly from ability, qualification, responsibility, invitation, or task existence. |
| Time sovereignty | The default control a person has over their own time. | Does not erase commitments, legitimate limited public task obligations, or repair duties, but these do not transfer ownership of all personal time. |
| Shared vulnerability | The fact that human capacity, health, and situation change across life; anyone may enter illness, disability, aging, incapacity, accident, or care need. | Does not rank sick, old, disabled, or dependent persons as lower. |
| Non-hierarchical mutual aid | Mutual support among equal subjects across time, persons, and life stages. | Not charity from higher to lower, not exact personal debt accounting, and not obedience owed by recipients. |
| Labor recognition | The requirement that labor, especially invisible or undervalued labor, be recognized as performed by subjects with time, judgment, risk, and knowledge. | Market price may evaluate exchange; it cannot define the worth of the worker. |
| Efficiency return | The principle that technical or organizational efficiency gains should not default to unilateral capture by organizations. | Specific formulas remain unsettled; distribution should consider shorter hours, rest, pay, safety, and public benefit. |
| Public return of innovation | The process by which an innovation from supported development, private convenience, or market demand becomes widely adopted and recognized as publicly necessary. | Public access does not erase creators, contributors, attribution, or proportionate limited return. |
| Exit | Withdrawal, expiration, termination, or closure of task participation, authority, organizational relation, or mechanism. | Ends future role and authority but does not erase prior harm, repair duties, or necessary safety handover. |
| Feedback | Information from affected persons, outcomes, and environmental change returning to decision and revision. | Cannot rely only on manager self-assessment or a single quantitative metric. |

## Frequently confused distinctions

### Need and legitimacy

Need as experience should be heard and expressible. Whether it creates responsibility for others or a public resource claim requires additional judgment.

### Responsibility and obedience

Responsibility traces effects, commitments, and repair. Obedience is a behavioral relation. Taking responsibility does not mean submitting to an identity or institution.

### Basic rights, factual power, authorized authority, and ability

Basic rights answer what a person has without task authorization. Factual power asks who actually controls what. Authorized authority asks who may create binding effects within what scope and duration. Ability asks whether and how someone can perform an action. These must not be substituted for one another.

### Public responsibility and task obligation

Public responsibility may begin with a recognized public need. A concrete task obligation requires additional procedures: institutional prior responsibility, fair distribution, capacity fit, reasonable conditions, alternatives, exemptions, appeal, and limited authorization.
