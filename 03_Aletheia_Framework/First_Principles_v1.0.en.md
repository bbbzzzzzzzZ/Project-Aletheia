# Aletheia First Principles v1.0

> This English document is a public-facing translation and summary of the frozen normative layer of Project Aletheia. The authoritative project record remains the Chinese [Aletheia 第一原则 v1.0 语义冻结](第一原则_v1.0_语义冻结.md) and the Chinese [第一原则](第一原则.md).

## What is frozen

Project Aletheia v1.0 freezes two parts of the Chinese First Principles document:

1. the eleven core axioms;
2. the forbidden crossings.

The freeze means that downstream theories, mechanisms, cases, lifecycle walkthroughs, legal interpretation drafts, and public writings must remain consistent with these principles. They may apply the principles in context, but they may not silently change their meaning for convenience, efficiency, or institutional habit.

The freeze does not mean that the principles have been empirically proven, legally adopted, publicly authorized, or successfully implemented.

## Eleven core axioms

### 1. Personhood precedes function

A person is not reducible to occupation, role, ability, contribution, family position, diagnosis, productivity, or institutional usefulness. These may describe a situation or qualification; they do not exhaust the person.

### 2. The person is the first speaker of their own need

A person has priority in expressing and interpreting their own lived experience and life purpose. This priority does not give them final authority over external facts, public resources, or other persons.

### 3. Need creates expression and request, not domination

Need gives a person standing to express, interpret, request, and act. It does not automatically create authority to command others, occupy public resources, or impose a task.

### 4. First determine need attribution, then account for responsibility

Before assigning responsibility, ask whose need the claim serves: the individual, an organization, a role-system, or the public. The person who suffers or speaks first is not automatically the subject to whom the need belongs.

### 5. Ability is only one condition of task qualification and authority

Real ability, evidence of ability, task qualification, willingness, obligation, and execution authority are distinct. Having ability does not mean being automatically assigned, and qualification does not itself authorize action.

### 6. Basic rights, factual power, and authorized authority must be separated

Basic rights do not depend on task authorization. Factual power can exist without formal authority. Authorized authority must be limited by procedure, scope, duration, oversight, and exit.

### 7. Personal time belongs to the person by default

No institution, employer, family, public system, or abstract goal owns a person’s time by default. Commitments and public responsibilities may limit time only within justified and bounded conditions.

### 8. Basic guarantees are not conditional on labor performance

Survival floors and basic rights are not rewards for work, contribution, obedience, or compliance. They protect the possibility of life, dissent, correction, and renewed participation.

### 9. Shared vulnerability must not become a hierarchy of persons

Illness, disability, aging, dependency, incapacity, and care needs are shared human possibilities. They must not create lower status, moral debt, or permanent subordination.

### 10. Public needs are borne by the public; individual mutual responsibility must be limited

When a need is properly recognized as public, the public bears first responsibility at the level of need attribution. This does not mean that every member has the same task. Individual contribution must be limited, fair, capacity-sensitive, reviewable, and protected by exemptions, alternatives, appeal, and exit.

### 11. Institutions must not define happiness for others

Institutions may protect conditions, coordinate conflict, maintain public systems, and prevent harm. They may not claim ownership over a person’s final life purpose.

## Forbidden crossings

The following moves are forbidden within the Aletheia framework:

- turning basic survival into a reward for obedience;
- turning help into permanent personal debt;
- turning contribution records into a rank of human worth;
- turning ability, expertise, wealth, or leadership into general authority over others;
- turning a public need into an automatic task for a particular person;
- turning a person’s suffering into proof that the person is first responsible for the whole system that should have responded;
- turning a private preference into a public obligation merely because it is expressed;
- turning temporary task authority into domain ownership;
- turning emergency authority into normal governance;
- turning factual control over information, data, budget, standards, agenda, or appeal into hidden unaccountable power;
- turning institutional convenience into a reason to erase affected persons, workers, dissenters, or those who bear cost.

## What is not frozen as v1.0

The following are not part of the frozen first principles:

- lifecycle scenarios such as birth, education, career transition, aging, and care;
- mechanisms for absence from public responsibility tasks;
- concrete standards for housing, welfare, childcare, or public services;
- reproductive and parental responsibility mechanisms;
- legal interpretation procedures;
- points, formulas, matrices, task durations, quotas, or ranking systems;
- transition plans, pilot designs, budgets, and real-world implementation stages.

These belong to downstream mechanism files, scenario walkthroughs, legal drafts, or validation records. They remain drafts unless separately confirmed and tested.

## Citation formula

When downstream documents use the first principles, they should say:

> This proposal is constrained by Aletheia First Principles v1.0. All roles, procedures, resources, and parameters in this document are downstream candidate mechanisms. They are not part of the frozen first principles and do not imply real-world implementation authority.
