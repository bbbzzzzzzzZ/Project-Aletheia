# Philosophical Foundation and Institutional Inference

> This English document translates and condenses the Chinese framework file `哲学理论基础与制度推论.md`. It does not modify the frozen First Principles v1.0 and does not treat the following empirical hypotheses or institutional inferences as proven facts, public authorization, or implementable mechanisms.

## 1. Four levels of claims

### 1.1 Value judgment

Aletheia understands the person as an end, not as material for institutions, organizations, markets, or other persons. Persons cooperate for survival, become responsible through needs, commitments, actions, and effects, and remain free beings with self-direction.

No one should have to be managed, bound, or coerced in order to survive. No person should acquire the power to use or sacrifice another person’s existence merely for private reasons.

A community may protect shared survival conditions, coordinate cooperation, and restrain harm. But it may not make survival itself conditional on obedience to an individual, organization, role, market, or abstract goal.

### 1.2 Descriptive judgment

Human needs are fluid. They change with time, body, environment, relationship, knowledge, and available resources. A person may not always know what they need, whether their interpretation is reasonable, or whether their proposed solution is possible.

Therefore, experiencing a need, expressing a need, interpreting a need, designing a way to satisfy it, justifying it publicly, and taking responsibility for consequences must not be collapsed into one step.

This descriptive claim does not itself create rights, duties, resources, or authority.

### 1.3 Empirical hypothesis

Aletheia’s working hypothesis is:

> If institutions secure the minimum conditions of shared survival without unifying individual needs, life purposes, or development paths, persons may be more likely to cooperate voluntarily, conflict may be reduced, and institutional stability may improve.

This is a hypothesis to be tested, not a proven law. It must be examined by asking who gains choice, who bears cost, who is omitted, whether coercion is reduced, whether cooperation is truly voluntary, and whether errors can be exited and repaired.

### 1.4 Institutional inference

Institutions should be organized around:

- shared survival as a floor;
- individual autonomy as protected space;
- voluntary cooperation, coordination, and incentive as ordinary mechanisms;
- rights boundaries and procedural justice as constraints;
- revisability as protection against error.

The goal is not to impose a unified form of life. The goal is to let persons avoid being managed, bound, or coerced merely to survive, while still enabling free cooperation for shared life.

This is a direction, not a full mechanism. Concrete agencies, resources, tasks, authorizations, compensations, and legal procedures remain downstream candidates.

## 2. Subjecthood, choice, and error

A weaker person does not need a complete, stable, or professionally articulated view before being a subject. Every person should retain the power to:

- choose whom to trust;
- accept an interpretation or definition;
- change that choice;
- question explanations;
- exit relationships where exit is legitimate and feasible;
- bear reasonable consequences for choices they understood and accepted.

Bearing consequences is not the same as personal-status punishment, loss of basic guarantees, loss of expression, loss of remedy, or loss of the right to choose again.

Stronger persons may have real wisdom, leadership, skill, and persuasive judgment. Voluntary following can be valid. But ability, reputation, and following do not automatically become permanent office, cross-domain authority, control over appeal, or ownership of another person’s life purpose.

The point is not to make strong persons powerless. It is to prevent strength from becoming ownership.

## 3. Common needs and public return of innovation

Aletheia gives priority to common needs, but this does not suppress individual development. “Common need” first protects shared survival, basic freedom, and public operation. It does not mean that every personal desire becomes public, nor that public systems may absorb all private life.

Innovation may arise from two paths:

1. externally collaborative development needs, where a person or team needs equipment, laboratories, funding, public materials, or organized support;
2. private convenience, consumption, market demand, play, laziness, or frustration.

The difference is not moral nobility. The difference is whether scarce external support is needed at the stage of creation.

The public return chain is:

```text
supported development need, private convenience, or market demand
-> invention, knowledge, technology, or organizational innovation
-> wider adoption
-> public necessity
-> public recognition through legitimate procedure
-> return into life, freedom, or public operation layers
-> limited and proportionate recognition for creators and contributors
```

Public recognition may require open knowledge, public procurement, public licensing, affordable supply, infrastructuralization, or public production. But public return must not become uncompensated seizure. If an innovation becomes necessary for shared life, creators and contributors should still receive authorship, recognition, and limited proportionate return.

## 4. Labor subjecthood and efficiency return

Workers are not merely instruments for producing outputs. They are subjects of work method, risk, time, evaluation, judgment, and improvement.

Institutions should actively respond when workers propose improvements in:

- work methods;
- professional analysis and judgment;
- working time and recovery;
- safety and responsibility boundaries;
- evaluation and correction.

Where safety, boundaries, and records are in place, workers should be able to try improved methods and organizational arrangements. They should also understand their own safety responsibilities, professional boundaries, third-party risks, and exit conditions.

Care, cleaning, maintenance, logistics, and similar work must not be undervalued because it is repetitive, invisible, or priced low by markets. Market price may evaluate exchange; it cannot decide the dignity of the worker.

Efficiency gains produced by technology or organization often depend on workers’ cooperation, experience, trial and error, and social knowledge. They should not default to unilateral organizational capture through unpaid intensification, hidden overtime, reduced staffing with unchanged burden, or surveillance.

The distribution of efficiency gains should be negotiated and, in public systems, publicly justified. Priority should be given to shorter working time, rest, improved pay, safer conditions, and public benefit. Specific formulas remain unsettled.

## 5. Conflict, scarcity, and irreversible harm

Institutions cannot eliminate all conflict. Needs conflict. Resources are scarce. People make mistakes. Experts disagree. Majorities may be wrong. Strong persons may be both useful and dangerous.

The institutional duty is to make conflict visible. A decision should state:

- its aim;
- evidence and uncertainty;
- affected parties;
- cost and opportunity cost;
- alternatives;
- compensation;
- correction path;
- conditions for suspension, rollback, or renewed decision.

When conflict cannot be fully resolved, Aletheia gives priority to avoiding irreversible, non-exitable, non-compensable, and domination-expanding harm. Majority support does not automatically make a decision just. Strong judgment does not automatically override the basic choice power of weaker persons.

## 6. Boundaries of this theory layer

This document does not provide:

- a unified life purpose for all persons;
- a proven theory of human nature;
- a concrete public service list;
- task hours, points, rotations, formulas, or assignment systems;
- fixed returns for innovation;
- a legal code;
- public authorization for real-world implementation.

The frozen First Principles v1.0 remain unchanged. This document is a downstream theoretical bridge and must not be used to rewrite the frozen text.
