# Project Aletheia

> **Project Aletheia studies how persons want to live, how persons can live together, and where institutions should intervene - and stop.**

`Aletheia` borrows from the Greek word `ἀλήθεια`, often associated with disclosure or unconcealment. The project does not try to tell people how they must live. It first asks what persons need, how those needs relate to others, and when shared coordination is genuinely required. Only then does it ask how need, responsibility, and power can remain bounded.

Project Aletheia is a basic inquiry into what might be called a civilizational operating grammar. It is not a social code that begins from one ready-made institutional blueprint.

## What the project studies

Project Aletheia does not start with a preferred political system, labor regime, legal code, or economic model. It starts with questions about persons and shared life:

- What is a person experiencing, and how do they want to live?
- How do relations with others support, limit, or harm that life?
- Which shared conditions deserve common maintenance?
- If stable coordination becomes necessary, what may an institution do, and where must it stop?

The first three questions do not automatically generate public tasks or personal obligations. A person’s difficulty may be answered by listening, interpretation, private action, relationship adjustment, refusal, withdrawal, or voluntary cooperation. Not entering an institutional process is not a failure.

Only when a matter separately satisfies a threshold for shared coordination does the project use its institutional grammar: who is affected, who bears cost, who has actual control, what authority is requested, how long it lasts, and how errors can be corrected.

## Seven public-facing core propositions

These seven propositions are the common public-language version used by the root README, the ten-minute overview, and public-facing essays. The full normative structure remains in the Chinese [First Principles](03_Aletheia_Framework/第一原则.md) and its [v1.0 semantic freeze](03_Aletheia_Framework/第一原则_v1.0_语义冻结.md).

1. **The person comes before identity and function.** Work, ability, and contribution describe only part of a person. They cannot rank persons or close off future change.
2. **A person is the first speaker of their own need, but need is not command.** Others may help interpret facts and consequences, but they do not own the person’s life purpose. Expressing a need does not automatically create authority over others.
3. **First ask whose need it is; then ask who did what.** Private needs are first attributed to the person whose life purpose they serve. Public needs, once properly recognized, belong first to the public. Expression, organization, commitment, execution, and harm must then be separately traced.
4. **Basic life is not exchanged for performance, and common responsibility must be limited and fair.** Basic guarantees are not rewards for obedience. Public need does not by itself assign a task to a particular person.
5. **Help is not charity, and receiving help is not a debt of personhood.** Real contributions should be recognized, but care cannot become permanent subordination.
6. **Ability, resources, or influence do not automatically authorize one person to decide for another.** Limited authority must specify object, scope, duration, oversight, and exit.
7. **Deciding what to do, who bears the task, and who may act are different steps; institutions must be revisable.** Rules must be able to change, pause, compensate, or end when evidence, burden, dissent, or harm shows that they are wrong.

## What Aletheia is not

- It is not a completed social blueprint ready for implementation.
- It is not a legal code.
- It is not an ideology that uses one philosopher, party, class, nation, or market as final authority.
- It is not a way to define happiness for everyone.
- It is not a system for ranking persons by labor, ability, contribution, or usefulness.

## Current status

Project Aletheia has clarified a philosophical direction and several boundaries. Many conflicts, distribution rules, oversight methods, fiscal questions, and implementation conditions remain unresolved.

The current English entry files are meant to make the project readable to international researchers. They should not be read as proof that the project has completed empirical validation, public authorization, legal drafting, or real-world institutional testing.
